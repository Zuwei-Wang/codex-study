---
name: study-weekly-review
description: Review a requested study period using Codex Study's recorded materials, task evidence, notes and explicit progress.
---

Read `study_snapshot`. Resolve the review period and workspace time zone; do not equate slide numbering with teaching week. Cover every recorded course, and explicitly state when a course has no current evidence. Separate known official dates, conflicts, personal plans, unknown dates and feedback windows.

Use the snapshot's user-provided learning preferences to shape the next suggested actions: small first steps and optional earlier milestones for `deadline-driven`, regular blocks for `steady`, adjustable blocks for `flexible`. Match self-study or class-led preparation/review, explicit block length and desired level of support. Unanswered, cleared or skipped preferences remain unknown. Ask about changing preferences only when relevant or requested; do not diagnose procrastination from an unfinished task, equate a preference with ability, or override today's request. Keep these recommendations separate from official dates; preferences never authorize reminders or scheduled runs.

Summarize recorded progress without inferring completion from missing tasks or from tool reads. Distinguish opened, read, drafted, uploaded, submitted and graded. Source verification and observation timestamps tell you when evidence was checked, not when the learner attended class. Flag partial/failed observations and older note citations if the current material hash has changed.

Use `study_read_material` only as needed to explain a recorded concept or produce requested revision practice. User learning reflections must be user-supplied. If the user asks to save the review, use `study_note_save` per course with exact material citations and distinguish your recommendations from source statements. A personal plan is not an official deadline. Generate `study_navigation` when helpful; do not schedule future runs or send reminders.
